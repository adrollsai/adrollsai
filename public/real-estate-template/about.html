We built a brokerage that would rather be right than agreeable

Sixteen years ago we watched a neighbour list their house 12% above what any comparable sale supported. It sat for five months and sold for less than the honest number would have achieved on day one. Everline exists to make sure that doesn't keep happening.

Our story

A small firm, deliberately

Amara Reyes founded Everline in 2009 after a decade in commercial development, with one hire and a borrowed desk on Alder Street. The proposition was simple: fewer clients, more attention, and an honest number even when the honest number costs us the instruction.

Sixteen years later we are still six advisors. That is not an accident of growth — it is the point. Each of our listing agents caps their active sellers at nine, which means we can afford to tell a client their house is worth less than they hoped, and still be there to sell it well.

The result, measured: 1,450 homes sold, $1.8 billion in volume, a 98% list-to-sale ratio, and 218 reviews averaging 4.9.

What we believe

Four rules we don't bend

These are not values on a wall. They are the things we have sacked clients over — and turned clients down for.

Tell the truth about the price

Every valuation comes with the three comparable sales that support it. If we think you should wait six months, we say that instead of taking the instruction.

One agent owns your file

The person who values your home is the person who negotiates your offer. No hand-offs to a coordinator the week it matters most.

Answer the phone

We commit to a one-business-hour reply, and we publish the number of the individual agent rather than a switchboard. It is a low bar that most of the industry misses.

Charge for the work, not the meeting

Valuations, buyer consultations and market reports are free. We are paid when a transaction completes, and never for a listing presentation.
